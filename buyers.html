Why it matters

The house is the easy part. The other ninety days are not.

Anyone can unlock a door. What you are actually hiring is judgment about price, condition, and what to do when the inspection comes back ugly and you have five days to decide.

Houston is not one market. A house in Cypress and a house in League City are priced by different pressures, sit in different flood and tax situations, and attract different buyers. Knowing which is which is the whole job.

We have seen how these go wrong, and most of the ways are avoidable if somebody catches them in the option period instead of at the closing table.

The part nobody explains well

How representation and payment actually work.

The rules changed and a lot of buyers are getting vague answers. Here is the plain version.

Before we tour homes together, Texas requires a written buyer representation agreement. It says who we work for — you — what we will do, how long it runs, and what our compensation is. You should read it, and we will walk you through every line before you sign.

Our fee is negotiable and it is stated in that agreement. In many transactions the seller still offers to cover some or all of it, but that is no longer automatic. We will tell you upfront what a given house means for your out-of-pocket cost, and we will ask for seller concessions where it makes sense.

What we will never do is let you find out about a cost at the closing table.

Straight answers

  • You sign before touring. That is the law now, not a sales tactic. It can be short-term or for a single property while you decide.
  • The fee is negotiable. There is no standard rate and anyone who tells you otherwise is wrong.
  • Seller-paid is common but not guaranteed. We check the terms on every listing before you fall in love with it.
  • You get the IABS form. It explains who represents whom. It is linked at the bottom of every page of this site.
  • Dual agency does not exist in Texas. If a situation gets complicated, we will explain intermediary status before it comes up, not after.

Local knowledge

What costs people money here

Five things that catch buyers moving to Houston, and plenty who already live here.

01 · Flood

The zone is not the whole story

Maps get redrawn and plenty of homes that flooded were not in a mapped zone at the time. We pull the history, not just the current designation.

02 · Taxes

MUD districts change the payment

Total rates swing meaningfully between neighboring subdivisions. A cheaper house in a high-rate district can cost more every month.

03 · Foundation

Gumbo clay moves

Some movement is normal here and some is expensive. Knowing the difference is worth more than any other inspection line item.

04 · Schools

Zones split subdivisions

The district is not the zone. We verify the specific campus before you write, because it can change resale.

05 · Deed restrictions

Houston has no zoning

Deed restrictions and HOA rules do the work zoning does elsewhere. They decide whether you can park the boat, run a shop, or build out back.

06 · New build

The builder's agent is the builder's

You can bring your own representation to a model home, and you should. Register us on your first visit or you may lose the option.

The variables

What actually determines your home’s value?

Location & neighborhood

Two homes with identical floor plans can differ by six figures across a boundary line. Street, block and immediate surroundings all carry weight.

Recent comparable sales

What genuinely similar homes closed for recently, adjusted for the ways yours differs. This is the backbone of any honest valuation.

Size & condition

Square footage matters, but so does how the space is arranged and what a buyer would want to change in the first year.

Lot & land

Acreage, usable land, frontage, trees, drainage and where the house sits on the plot. Outside Houston proper this often moves the number most.

Upgrades & features

Some improvements return most of their cost and some return very little. Which is which depends on the price band and the buyer.

Market demand

How many buyers are looking in your price range right now, and how fast homes are leaving the market. This shifts quarter to quarter.

School district

Attendance zones shape buyer pools, particularly for four-bedroom homes. Zoning can and does change, so it is worth confirming rather than assuming.

Economic conditions

Mortgage rates, local hiring and construction costs all reach the price a buyer can pay, whatever the house is like.

Current competition

What a buyer sees alongside your home the weekend it lists. Three similar homes nearby changes the strategy entirely.

Local market

Home values across Greater Houston

“Houston home values” is not really one market. The metro spans roughly ten thousand square miles and a home in the Heights, a home in Cypress and a home on five acres north of Magnolia are priced by three different sets of buyers using three different sets of comparables. An estimate that averages across that spread will be wrong in a specific and predictable way.

Inside the loop and the close-in suburbs, values move mostly on location and condition. Lots are small and similar, so what separates two houses is the street, the school zone, and how much a buyer would have to spend in the first year. Comparable sales are plentiful, which is why automated estimates tend to be closest here — and why the difference between a good listing strategy and an average one still runs into real money.

Move north and the arithmetic changes. In The Woodlands and the master-planned communities around it, the village, the section and even the phase matter, because inventory within one community can be nearly identical and buyers compare directly. In Spring and Tomball, a mature subdivision and a new-construction section a mile apart compete for different buyers at different prices, and a builder’s incentives on the new one quietly cap what the resale beside it can ask.

Further out, lot size starts to carry the number. Around Magnolia and Montgomery, acreage, usable land, road frontage, trees and whether the property is on a well or a MUD system can matter as much as the house. Automated models are weakest here, because there are fewer genuine comparables and the ones they find are often not comparable at all. On and around Lake Conroe, waterfront adds a further layer — bulkhead condition, water depth, boathouse, and open water versus a cove can separate two otherwise similar homes considerably.

Conroe sits across several of these at once: established neighborhoods near the older part of town, new construction pushing outward, and acreage beyond that. A single ZIP-code average there tells a homeowner almost nothing.

Three things move values everywhere in this region regardless of where you sit: how much comparable inventory is competing with you the week you list, what condition your home is in relative to that competition, and whether recent nearby sales were genuinely similar or simply nearby. A valuation that does not address all three is a guess with a decimal point.

Common questions

Before you ask

How is my home’s value calculated?

By comparison, not by formula. We start with homes near yours that recently sold and were genuinely similar, then adjust for the ways yours differs — size, condition, lot, updates, position. Then we look at what is competing with you right now, because a fair value in a quiet month is not a fair value when four similar homes list the same week.

Is the home valuation really free?

Yes, with no cost and no obligation. It is how we introduce ourselves, and a good number of the people we prepare one for are not selling this year.

Do I have to list my home with TwelvePoint?

No. You are not agreeing to anything by asking. If you decide to sell and want to work with someone else, or not to sell at all, that is a perfectly normal outcome and we would rather you had good information either way.

How accurate are online home value estimates?

It depends heavily on where you are. In a dense subdivision with many recent, similar sales they can land close. On acreage, waterfront, or anything unusual they are often well off, because the model is comparing your property to homes that are not really comparable. They also cannot see condition or what you have updated.

How long does it take to receive my report?

Usually within one business day. If your property is unusual — significant acreage, waterfront, or something with limited comparables — it can take a little longer, and we will tell you rather than rush it.

What information do you need from me?

The address is enough to start. Square footage, bedroom and bathroom count, condition and any renovations make the range tighter, but leave blank anything you are unsure of. We would rather have a gap than a guess.

Can you value acreage properties?

Yes, and it is one of the places a human valuation earns its keep. Usable land, road frontage, fencing, water, whether you are on a well or a MUD system, and how the house sits on the plot all move the number in ways an automated model does not attempt.

Can you value luxury homes?

Yes. Higher price bands have fewer comparable sales, so the analysis leans harder on finish level, architectural quality and who the realistic buyer is. A wider range with the reasoning shown is more honest than a precise-looking number with nothing behind it.

Can you value waterfront property?

Yes — around Lake Conroe in particular. Bulkhead condition, water depth, boathouse, and open water versus a cove can separate two otherwise similar homes considerably, and those factors rarely appear in public records at all.

Do renovations increase my home’s value?

Some do, some barely register, and a few can narrow your buyer pool. It depends on the work, the price band and what buyers in your neighborhood expect as standard. Where it is relevant, the report flags which work is worth doing before listing and which is not.

What if I’m not ready to sell yet?

That is the most common reason people ask. Knowing where you stand is useful for planning a move, weighing a refinance, deciding whether to renovate, or protesting your property taxes. Tell us your timeline on the form and we will treat it accordingly.

How often should I check my home’s value?

Once a year is sensible for most homeowners, and sooner if something changes — a major renovation, a run of sales on your street, or a shift in rates. Values in this market can move meaningfully inside twelve months.

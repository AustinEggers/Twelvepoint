What your house is worth versus what the website says

Selling 4 March 2026 6 min read By Austin Eggers

Every automated valuation tool works the same way: public records in, estimate out. Square footage, bed and bath count, lot size, and whatever nearby sales the algorithm can find. It is a reasonable starting point and a poor stopping point.

The problem is not the math. The problem is that the model has never been inside your house, and in Houston the inside is where most of the value gets decided.

What the model cannot see

It does not know you replaced the roof after the last hailstorm, or that the house two streets over sold cheap because of a foundation issue that yours does not have. It does not know your section of the subdivision backs to a greenbelt and the rest does not.

  • Condition and updates, which are invisible in county records
  • Flood history as opposed to current flood zone
  • The specific school attendance zone, which can split a single neighborhood
  • What is actively competing with you this weekend
Two identical houses a mile apart can carry very different monthly payments. Buyers shop payments.

Where Houston is different

Total tax rates swing meaningfully between neighboring MUD districts. A cheaper house in a high-rate district can cost more every month than a pricier one down the road, and that difference shows up in what a buyer is willing to offer.

The short version

Use the online number to decide whether to have a conversation. Do not use it to set a list price, and definitely do not use it to decide whether selling makes sense.

If you want the real number, we will put a written analysis together — comparable sales with adjustments shown, what is actively competing, a recommended range, and a net sheet. Free, and no listing pitch attached.

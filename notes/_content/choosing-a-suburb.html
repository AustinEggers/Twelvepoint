Buyers arriving in Houston are handed the same handful of names — The Woodlands, Cypress, Katy, Conroe, Sugar Land, Pearland — and left to work out the difference from a map. The names are not the useful unit. Each covers a large area containing neighborhoods that have very little in common with one another.

What separates two houses is rarely the suburb they sit in.

Four things that actually differ

The total tax rate. Covered in more detail elsewhere on this page, but it belongs at the top of any comparison. Two homes at the same price in different districts are not the same purchase.

The school attendance zone, not the district. Districts here are large and the schools inside them are not interchangeable. A single subdivision can be split between two elementary zones. Zones are also redrawn as districts grow, so "zoned to" is a present-tense statement.

The actual commute, at the actual hour. Not the map estimate. Drive it at the time you would really drive it, in the direction you would really go. Houston traffic is directional and the difference between 7am and 10am is not small.

How built-out it is. An area still under construction means ongoing noise and traffic, but also a builder actively competing with any resale you might later list. An established area means mature trees and a settled tax rate.

Ask what this neighborhood will be like in five years, not just what it is like on a Saturday morning.

The visit that tells you most

Go back at a different time. A neighborhood on a Sunday afternoon and the same neighborhood at 6pm on a Tuesday are different places. So is a street in February versus one in August, when the drainage gets tested.

Look for what a daytime visit hides: where people park, how close the nearest arterial road really is, whether the retail nearby is thriving or emptying.

What to ask an agent who knows the area

  • What is the total tax rate here, and is it rising or falling?
  • Which schools is this specific address zoned to today, and is a rezoning expected?
  • What is being built within a mile, and what will it do to traffic?
  • How long do homes here take to sell compared with a mile away?
  • Has this section had drainage problems?

If the answers are vague, that is information too.

The short version

Do not shop by suburb name. Shop by tax rate, attendance zone, the commute you will actually drive, and what the area will look like once the construction stops.

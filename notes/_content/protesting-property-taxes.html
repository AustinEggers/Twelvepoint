Every spring your appraisal district sends a Notice of Appraised Value with a number on it. That number is an estimate produced at scale, largely by model, for hundreds of thousands of properties at once. It has never been inside your house.

You are allowed to disagree, and disagreeing is more routine than most homeowners realise.

The deadline first

15 May, or 30 days after your notice was mailed, whichever falls later. There is no grace period and no late filing. If you miss it, the value stands for the year regardless of how wrong it is.

File the protest even if you are unsure whether you will pursue it. Filing preserves the right; you can always withdraw.

Two grounds worth knowing

You can argue that the market value is too high — your home would not sell for what they say. You can also argue unequal appraisal, which says your home is valued higher than comparable properties around it regardless of what it might sell for.

The second is often the stronger argument and the one homeowners skip. Being taxed more than a near-identical house two streets away is a problem on its own terms.

You are not arguing that your house is bad. You are arguing that a number produced by a model, at volume, missed something specific.

What actually helps

  • Photographs of condition. Dated finishes, a roof near the end of its life, foundation movement, deferred work. The model assumes average condition.
  • Repair estimates. A written quote for work the house needs is concrete in a way that description is not.
  • Comparable sales that sold below your assessed value, close by, similar in size and age.
  • Your closing statement, if you bought recently below the assessed value. Hard to argue with.

What does not help: that your taxes went up, that you cannot afford the bill, or that the increase feels unfair. Those are real, but they are not grounds.

How it unfolds

Most protests never reach a hearing. You file, and the district offers an informal settlement — often a modest reduction, accepted or declined on the spot. Take it if it is fair. If not, you go to a formal hearing before the Appraisal Review Board, which is less intimidating than it sounds: a short presentation, your evidence, their evidence, a decision.

Bring printed copies. Be specific and unemotional. Panels hear a great deal of grievance and very little evidence, and evidence is what moves them.

On hiring someone

Firms will protest on your behalf for a share of the savings. That is reasonable value if you will not otherwise do it, and it is worth knowing the alternative is a form and an afternoon. Neither choice is wrong — not protesting at all is the one that costs money.

The short version

Diary 15 May. File. Photograph what is wrong with the house. Argue unequal appraisal as well as market value.

If you are protesting and need comparable sales, ask — pulling them is straightforward for us and useful to you. Point twelve of our standard is that the relationship does not end at the closing table.

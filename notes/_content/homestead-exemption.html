If you bought a home in Texas and it is your principal residence, you are entitled to a homestead exemption. It removes a slice of your home's value from taxation, it is free to file, and a surprising number of people never get round to it.

It matters more this year than it used to. Proposition 13 raised the general school-district exemption to $140,000 for the 2026 tax year, up from $100,000. Homeowners who are 65 or older, or who have a qualifying disability, receive an additional $60,000 on top.

The part people underestimate

The exemption is the headline, but the cap is often worth more over time. Once a homestead exemption is in place, the taxable value of your home cannot rise by more than 10% a year regardless of what the market does.

In a stretch of rapid appreciation that ceiling does more for your bill than the exemption itself, and it compounds. It is also the reason filing in your first eligible year matters — the protection starts when the exemption does.

Free, once, ten minutes. There is no version of this that is not worth doing.

Deadlines, and the one that catches people

The general filing deadline is 30 April. The detail that trips people up is that your Texas driver's licence must show the property address by then — the address on your licence has to match the home you are claiming. Updating it is quick, but not if you discover the requirement on 29 April.

The other date worth knowing is 15 May, or 30 days after your Notice of Appraised Value arrives, whichever is later. That is the deadline to protest your valuation, and it is unforgiving. There is no late filing and no appeal — miss it and you wait a year.

How to file

  • Go to your county appraisal district — HCAD for Harris, MCAD for Montgomery, and so on
  • Find the residence homestead exemption application
  • File online where offered; most districts allow it
  • Check your licence address first

You do not need to pay anyone to do this. Companies that offer to file it for a fee are charging for a free form.

The short version

File it the first year you are eligible. Check your driver's licence address before 30 April. Diary 15 May for the protest deadline, whether or not you intend to use it.

Exemption amounts and rules change with the legislature — the 2026 figures above reflect Proposition 13. Confirm the current numbers with your appraisal district, and treat this as general information rather than tax advice.

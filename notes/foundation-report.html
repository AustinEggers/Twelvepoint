Reading a Houston foundation report without panicking

Buying August 2026 6 min read

Houston sits on clay that swells when it rains and shrinks when it does not. Foundations move. Almost every slab in this region has moved to some degree, and an inspection report that mentions movement is describing the local geology as much as the house.

That is not a reason to relax. It is a reason to read carefully instead of reacting to the word.

What the report is and is not

A general home inspector is not a structural engineer. They will note what they observe — sloping floors, cracked brick, doors out of square — and, when it warrants it, recommend evaluation by an engineer. That recommendation is a referral, not a verdict.

If you want an actual opinion on whether the foundation needs work, that comes from a licensed engineer, in writing, for a few hundred dollars. On a house showing real symptoms it is among the best money you can spend during an option period.

The question is never "has it moved". It is whether it is still moving, and what that has done to the rest of the house.

Ordinary versus worth investigating

Broadly, cosmetic evidence of past movement is common. Evidence of ongoing movement, or of movement severe enough to affect how the house functions, is not.

Things that usually turn out to be ordinary:

  • Hairline cracks in drywall at door and window corners
  • Small stair-step cracks in exterior brick mortar
  • A little slope across a large room

Things worth an engineer:

  • Cracks you can fit a coin into, or that run through brick rather than mortar
  • Doors and windows that will not close, or gaps above them
  • Separation where walls meet ceilings
  • Visible slope you can feel walking across it
  • Any evidence of previous repair — which raises different questions

If it has been repaired before

Previous foundation work is not automatically bad. Piers correctly installed by a reputable company, with documentation and a transferable warranty, can leave a house in better shape than an untouched one.

Ask for the invoice, the engineer's report from the time, and confirmation that the warranty transfers. If none of that exists, you are being asked to take the repair on trust.

Watering, unglamorously

The most effective foundation maintenance in Houston is keeping soil moisture around the slab even — consistent watering in drought rather than letting the clay dry out and then soaking it. It is dull advice and it prevents more damage than anything else available to a homeowner.

The short version

Movement is expected. Ongoing or functional damage is not. When the report recommends an engineer, get one during the option period, while walking away is still free.

This is general guidance, not an engineering opinion. Any specific house needs a licensed engineer looking at that specific slab — we will point you at ones we have actually used.

The agent at the model home does not work for you

New Construction August 2026 5 min read

Walk into a model home on a Saturday and someone will greet you warmly, answer every question, and walk you through the floor plans. They will be genuinely helpful and probably very good at their job.

They also represent the builder. That is not a criticism — it is their role, disclosed and entirely proper. It just means that in a negotiation about price, upgrades or contract terms, there is nobody in the room whose duty runs to you.

What that changes in practice

Anything you say to the builder's representative is information the builder has. How much you can stretch, how quickly you need to move, whether you have another community in mind, whether you have sold your current house yet — all of it is ordinary conversation, and all of it is useful to the other side.

A buyer's agent is the person you can think out loud in front of.

The builder pays for representation either way. The only question is whether any of it is yours.

The timing thing that catches people out

This is the part worth reading twice. Most builders require that your agent be present or registered on your first visit to the community. Not the second visit. Not before you sign. The first.

Turn up alone, sign the visitor card, and come back a fortnight later with an agent, and the builder may decline to recognise them. At that point you either proceed without representation or start again somewhere else.

Policies differ between builders and sometimes between communities of the same builder, which is precisely why it is not worth guessing. If you are even curious about a community, tell us before you drive out. Registering takes a minute.

What it costs you

In new construction the builder has a marketing budget that anticipates buyer representation. Bringing your own agent does not generally raise the price of the house, and builders very rarely discount for unrepresented buyers — the incentive structure is built around the sales price they publish.

So the practical choice is not "pay for an agent or save money". It is whether you use representation that has already been accounted for.

What we actually do on a new build

  • Read the builder's contract, which is theirs rather than a TREC form and is written for them
  • Compare the incentive against what the lender credit actually costs over the loan
  • Tell you which upgrades hold value at resale and which do not
  • Attend the walkthrough and keep a list the builder has to answer
  • Chase warranty items after closing, when the sales office has moved on

The short version

Call before the first visit, not after. It is the cheapest thing you will do in the whole process and the only step that cannot be undone later.

Registration policies vary by builder and community, and they change. If you are planning to walk a model home this weekend, send us the community name first — a text is enough.
